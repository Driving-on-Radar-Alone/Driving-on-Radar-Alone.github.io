Abstract

Under continuous registration to a fixed prior map, a momentary registration degradation does not stay inside the estimator: it leaves as a pose jump in the absolute pose stream and arrives at the controller as an input. This work integrates one forward 4D imaging radar with a radar-native voxel prior map and drives an on-road passenger car in closed loop on that pose alone. Online the radar is the only exteroceptive and ego-motion sensor — no LiDAR, camera, IMU, or wheel-speed and yaw-rate CAN enters localization or motion estimation, and GNSS supplies a single initial pose at trip start.

A fixed-lag smoother couples Doppler-GICP registration against the immutable map with a constant-velocity motion model, and the resulting absolute map-frame pose is handed to an otherwise unchanged planning and control stack whose degraded-mode profile adjusts speed cap and headway only. Rather than ranking sensor modalities, the evaluation asks which sensor configuration equivalent stability requires: four localization arms — a GNSS/INS reference, the radar-only system, a KISS-ICP first-stage steel-man, and a LiDAR prior-map particle filter carrying CAN wheel-speed odometry — are read through pose-jump, jitter and steering-transfer indices alongside localization accuracy and closed-loop path following, over seven scenarios at the K-City proving ground.

Introduction

Radar odometry and SLAM on 4D imaging radar, and localization on radar-native maps with 360° spinning 2D radar, are both established lines of work. They target odometry or SLAM against a growing local map, or open-loop per-frame localization against a map built offline. Continuously publishing an absolute pose against an immutable prior map, and measuring the stability of that pose while it closes the control loop, is a distinct problem.

It is also a different measurement. Assessing output smoothness separately from accuracy is established practice, but the radar prior-map localizers closest to this setting report localization RMSE and no pose-jump, jitter or steering metric. Meanwhile the established convention keeps discrete map-frame corrections out of the control-facing output entirely. Here the controller consumes the absolute map-constrained pose directly, so pose jumps and their transfer into the steering command become the quantities to measure.

An industry safety framework places a stop or pull-over fallback after critical sensor failure without naming radar a localization modality, and on-road passenger-car radar localization has been reported open-loop. The integration problem addressed here is therefore not radar closed-loop driving in general.

  • Integration A 4D imaging radar, a radar-native fixed prior map, an on-road passenger vehicle and radar-only degraded-mode operation, verified at vehicle level.
  • Output A control-consumable absolute pose, with pose jumps and their transfer to a fixed controller measured rather than deferred to another coordinate frame.
  • Evaluation Four localization arms on one vehicle and one controller, read through closed-loop stability indices instead of localization error alone.

Out of scope. Map building, the fixed-lag smoother itself, and the reused residuals and optimizer are premises, not contributions. The paper claims no general absence of radar closed-loop driving and no accuracy advantage in adverse weather. Fault detection, arbitration and protection-level integrity guarantees are excluded; the mode-switch signal is injected externally, and the closed-loop evaluation runs each route entirely in radar-only mode.

Method

Three regions stay separated: offline preparation of the given prior maps, online radar-only operation, and the evaluation-only configurations. Along the online path the inputs are 20 Hz scans of one forward 4D imaging radar — corner radars are not used — the fixed radar voxel map, the lanelet map, and a single GNSS-grade initial pose at trip start. GNSS serves only that initial pose. Wheel-speed and yaw-rate CAN are not localization or motion-estimation inputs; the actuator-interface CAN carried on the control output is not a localization input either.

The evaluation-only configurations — GNSS/INS, LiDAR-only, and LiDAR prior-map matching — share no connection with the operating path.

Control-consumable localization

Localization registers each scan to a fixed map that is neither grown nor reloaded — the invariant exogenous map frame is the reference of the published pose.

Fixed-lag window

Poses and body velocities over a sliding window solve one MAP problem: a unary map-registration constraint per scan against the fixed map, plus a binary constant-velocity constraint between consecutive states, under a robust kernel and solved with g2o in SE(2). The design hypothesis is that optimizing the two jointly reduces the direct transfer of a single degraded registration into a single pose update.

Doppler-GICP against a voxel map

Each map voxel stores a point count, an RCS-weighted mean and a covariance, built offline from radar returns accumulated along a post-processed GNSS/INS trajectory. The geometric residual enters as a Mahalanobis term with the combined point–voxel covariance; the Doppler residual constrains ego velocity and yaw rate from the radial velocities of static returns, so velocity is never obtained by differencing positions.

Per-scan cycle

Predict from constant velocity; add the state node and its constant-velocity edge; attach the scan to the newest node and register it to the map with Doppler-GICP; optimize all fixed-map and motion edges jointly; erase nodes beyond the window and publish. Only the newest node is registered per cycle — past scans are not re-registered. The window holds 21 vertices, optimizes over 20, and erases at 100 m.

At standstill the near-zero radial velocities of static returns act as a zero-velocity constraint. Window poses are not hard-fixed: the fixed-map unary edges provide the gauge.

Radar-only degraded mode

In normal mode the camera observes the lane. In degraded mode the lanelet map alone supplies lane and path geometry.

One interface changes

The lanelet path is projected into the vehicle frame using the radar voxel map's absolute pose to form the local path ahead. The existing stack — map loading, global, behavior and trajectory planning, and control — is algorithmically unchanged; the only interface changed in operation is the source of the vehicle-state bridge, and the degraded-mode profile adjusts planning parameters only, namely speed cap and headway.

Why both errors are measured

By design a pose error appears as lateral error of the projected path, and a pose step as a step in the path and in the steering command. That correlation is not treated as established causation, so localization accuracy and closed-loop path following are evaluated together, and map-frame localization error and path-following cross-track error stay separate quantities throughout.

A parallel detection path runs an independent radar ego-velocity estimator, static/dynamic separation and a multi-object tracker, taking only ego state from localization. Multi-object tracking performance is out of scope.

Test site and operational design domain

All runs took place at K-City, an autonomous-driving proving ground in Hwaseong-si, Republic of Korea.

The seven scenarios are laid over four domains of the course. The urban block grid and its arterials carry lane keeping and the right- and left-turn scenarios; the roundabout and the expressway section are driven as their own scenarios; and the roofed corridor is the GNSS-shadowed zone, entered with a roundabout U-turn. The full-course scenario chains all of them into a single 4.78 km drive.

The GNSS/INS reference is graded by its RTK-fixed status, so runs recorded without RTK correction serve stability, completion and intervention counts only, never absolute accuracy.

Experiments

The platform is an IONIQ 6-class passenger car with one ARS548 4D imaging radar on the K-City closed test track. Two evaluation sessions used the same vehicle and controller as nonconcurrent separate runs: 2026-09-09 for the KISS-ICP arm, and 2026-09-11 for the radar-only arm, the LiDAR prior-map arm and the GNSS/INS reference runs. The question is narrower than a modality ranking — it asks which sensor configuration equivalent stability requires — and the steel-man is staged from L1 to L2, whose added CAN wheel-speed and yaw-rate odometry are exactly the signals radar Doppler replaces in R.

Preregistered bounds

Bounds were fixed before the runs, from a 3.0 m lane width and a 20 m minimum curvature radius: a geometric alert limit of 0.46 m, a 0.50° heading allocation leaving 0.43 m, and division by 2.92 giving 0.15 m.

Longitudinal and heading errors are reported as p95 and max without borrowing either lateral bound, and no p95 is compared with a max bound. No protection level is computed, so this compares measured error against a preregistered error budget rather than guaranteeing integrity.

What is measured

Pose jump

The innovation against the constant-velocity prediction, decomposed into longitudinal, lateral and yaw components in the vehicle frame with priority on lateral: exceedances per kilometre at 0.1 m and 0.2 m, the largest single jump, and velocity spikes, with no filtering before differencing.

Jitter

The 1–5 Hz power spectral density of estimated velocity and heading. The band is an operational definition of this work, chosen to separate oscillatory energy above the intended path-following input band, with a band-independent frequency-weighted auxiliary index over the whole steering spectrum.

Transfer to control

Two primary indices from the logged steering command of the fixed controller — its 1–5 Hz band PSD and its rate p95 — plus the measured steering-wheel-angle rate p95 as an auxiliary index only. The two are not merged: the measured angle adds actuator dynamics.

One sample is the p95 over the time series of one run or one localization-source segment; a run recorded as four logs counts as one run. Runs are aligned across sessions by scenario and speed, and no statistical test is applied — with one or two runs per cell the statistics are asymmetric and descriptive.
